3e:T1051,<p>Sakkarai pongal is the dish that gives the Pongal festival its name. Rice and a little moong dal are cooked until soft and creamy, then stirred into melted jaggery with plenty of ghee, cardamom, cashews and raisins. It is rich, fragrant and a lovely golden brown. Every family has its own way of making it; this version is simple enough for a first attempt and tastes like the one you remember from home.</p>
<h2 id="ingredients">Ingredients</h2>
<ul>
<li>¾ cup raw rice (pachai arisi, such as raw ponni or sona masuri)</li>
<li>¼ cup split yellow moong dal (paasi paruppu)</li>
<li>3 cups water</li>
<li>½ cup milk (optional, for a creamier pongal)</li>
<li>1¼ cups powdered or grated jaggery (about 250 g)</li>
<li>¼ cup water, for melting the jaggery</li>
<li>4 tablespoons ghee, divided</li>
<li>12 cashews, halved</li>
<li>1 tablespoon raisins</li>
<li>½ teaspoon cardamom powder (from about 5 pods)</li>
<li>A tiny pinch of edible camphor (pachai karpooram), optional</li>
<li>A pinch of salt</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="method">Method</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Roast the dal.</strong> Warm a heavy pan over medium heat and dry-roast the moong dal for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until it smells nutty and turns a shade darker. Do not let it brown.</li>
<li><strong>Wash.</strong> Tip the dal into a bowl with the rice and rinse two or three times until the water runs mostly clear.</li>
<li><strong>Cook the rice and dal.</strong> Put the rice and dal in a pressure cooker with the 3 cups of water and the milk, if using. Cook for 5 to 6 whistles on medium heat, then let the pressure release on its own. To cook in a pot instead, simmer everything covered on low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring often and adding a splash of hot water if it catches, until the rice is completely soft.</li>
<li><strong>Mash.</strong> Open the cooker and mash the rice and dal lightly with the back of a ladle so it becomes soft and creamy.</li>
<li><strong>Melt the jaggery.</strong> In a separate pan, heat the jaggery with ¼ cup water, stirring until it dissolves completely. Pour it through a fine sieve to catch any grit, then return the syrup to the pan and let it bubble for 2 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Combine.</strong> Pour the jaggery syrup into the mashed rice. Cook on low heat, stirring all the time, for 5 to 7 minutes until the mixture thickens and comes together. Stir in 2 tablespoons of ghee, the cardamom powder, the pinch of salt and the edible camphor, if using.</li>
<li><strong>Fry the nuts.</strong> Heat the remaining 2 tablespoons of ghee in a small pan. Fry the cashews until golden, then add the raisins and stir until they puff up. Pour the ghee, cashews and raisins over the pongal and mix gently.</li>
<li><strong>Serve.</strong> Serve warm. The pongal thickens as it cools, so loosen it with a little warm milk if needed.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="tips">Tips</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Jaggery varies.</strong> Some jaggery is much sweeter than others. Start with 1 cup, taste after mixing, and add more if you like it sweeter.</li>
<li><strong>Always strain the jaggery.</strong> Even good jaggery often has bits of sand or fibre in it.</li>
<li><strong>Edible camphor only.</strong> Use food-grade pachai karpooram from an Indian grocer, never the camphor tablets used for aarti. Just a few grains are enough; too much tastes bitter.</li>
<li><strong>Be generous with ghee.</strong> Sakkarai pongal is meant to be rich. Ghee gives the gloss and aroma, so do not skimp.</li>
<li><strong>Keep it soft.</strong> The pongal should be loose enough to spoon, not set firm. Stop cooking when it still looks a little softer than you want.</li>
<li><strong>Leftovers</strong> keep in the fridge for 2 days. Warm them with a splash of milk.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="when-its-made">When it's made</h2>
<p>Sakkarai pongal is cooked on the first day of Thai, the Pongal festival, when families boil fresh rice with milk and jaggery in a new pot and call out "Pongalo pongal!" as it bubbles over, a sign of plenty for the year ahead. It is also a favourite offering for many festivals and temple days.
