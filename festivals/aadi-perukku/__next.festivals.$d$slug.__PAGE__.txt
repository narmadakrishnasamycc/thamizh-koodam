3:T1948,<p>Aadi Perukku (ஆடிப்பெருக்கு) is a festival of thanks to water. <em>Perukku</em> means "rising" or "swelling". In the Tamil month of Aadi, monsoon rains fill the rivers of Tamil Nadu, especially the Kaveri (காவிரி), and on the eighteenth day of the month families gather on the riverbanks to thank the river as a mother who feeds the land. Because of the date, the festival is also called Pathinettam Perukku (பதினெட்டாம் பெருக்கு), "the rising of the eighteenth". It is a cheerful day of prayer, picnics and family time by the water.</p>
<h2 id="when-is-it-celebrated">When is it celebrated</h2>
<p>Aadi Perukku falls on Aadi 18, which is usually 2 or 3 August. The month of Aadi (ஆடி) runs from about mid-July to mid-August, and it is a special month in Tamil homes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Aadi Fridays</strong>, <em>Aadi Velli</em> (ஆடி வெள்ளி), are dedicated to the Goddess, and <a href="/deities/amman/">Amman</a> temples are especially busy.</li>
<li><strong>Aadi Amavasai</strong> (ஆடி அமாவாசை), the new moon, is an important day for remembering ancestors.</li>
<li><strong>Aadi Pooram</strong> (ஆடிப்பூரம்) celebrates the birth star of the poet-saint Andal, a devotee of <a href="/deities/vishnu/">Vishnu</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>By tradition, many families avoid weddings and housewarmings in Aadi, keeping the month for prayer and worship instead.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-is-celebrated">Why it is celebrated</h2>
<p>Every year, the south-west monsoon brings heavy rain to the Western Ghats, where the Kaveri begins, at Talakaveri in the Kodagu (Coorg) hills of Karnataka. By Aadi, the river is running full as it flows across Tamil Nadu to its delta around Thanjavur, often called the rice bowl of Tamil Nadu. For farmers, this water means a new season of planting. An old Tamil proverb says <em>"Aadi pattam thedi vidhai"</em> (ஆடிப் பட்டம் தேடி விதை) — "seek the Aadi season to sow your seeds".</p>
<p>For Tamils, the river is not just water but a mother, lovingly called Kaveri Thaai (காவிரித் தாய்). The Tamil epic Silappathikaram (சிலப்பதிகாரம்) even includes songs in praise of the Kaveri. Aadi Perukku is a way of saying thank you for the water that grows food, fills wells and keeps life going.</p>
<p>According to a popular legend, the sage Agathiyar (அகத்தியர்) once carried the waters of the Kaveri in his small water pot, his <em>kamandalam</em> (கமண்டலம்). <a href="/deities/ganesha/">Ganesha</a>, in the form of a crow, tipped the pot over, and the water flowed out to become the river, bringing life to the land.</p>
<h2 id="how-families-celebrate">How families celebrate</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Going to the river</strong> — families head to the steps along the riverbank, called <em>padithurai</em> (படித்துறை), usually in the morning, carrying food, flowers and pooja items.</li>
<li><strong>A simple pooja</strong> — on the bank, they light a lamp, offer flowers, turmeric, kumkum and fruit, and pray to the river. In some places, little lamps are floated on the water.</li>
<li><strong>Gifts for the river</strong> — some families offer <em>kaadholai karugamani</em> (காதோலை கருகமணி), palm-leaf ear ornaments and black beads, just as one would give gifts to a beloved woman of the family.</li>
<li><strong>Yellow threads</strong> — married women often tie a new <em>manjal kayiru</em> (மஞ்சள் கயிறு), a thread dyed with turmeric, and in many families girls and others tie yellow threads too, with prayers for a happy family life.</li>
<li><strong>Newly married couples</strong> — many visit the river together on this day, and in some families they float their dried wedding garlands on the water.</li>
<li><strong>A riverside picnic</strong> — the food is first offered to the river and then shared with family, friends and neighbours.</li>
</ul>
<p>Families who live far from a river often pray at a nearby lake or temple tank, or at home before a pot of fresh water decorated with turmeric, kumkum and flowers.</p>
<h2 id="traditional-food">Traditional food</h2>
<p>Aadi Perukku is famous for <em>kalavai sadham</em> (கலவை சாதம்), also called <em>chithrannam</em> (சித்ரான்னம்): different kinds of flavoured rice that are easy to pack and carry to the riverbank.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lemon rice</strong> — <em>elumichai sadham</em> (எலுமிச்சை சாதம்)</li>
<li><strong>Tamarind rice</strong> — <em>puliyodharai</em> (புளியோதரை)</li>
<li><strong>Coconut rice</strong> — <em>thengai sadham</em> (தேங்காய் சாதம்)</li>
<li><strong>Curd rice</strong> — <em>thayir sadham</em> (தயிர் சாதம்)</li>
<li><strong>Sweet pongal</strong> — many families also make a sweet dish such as <a href="/recipes/sakkarai-pongal/">sakkarai pongal</a> as an offering.</li>
</ul>
<p>Crisp vadai, appalam and pickle make the picnic complete.</p>
<h2 id="kolam-and-decorations">Kolam and decorations</h2>
<p>As on other special days, a fresh kolam (கோலம்) is drawn at the doorstep in the morning. Children might enjoy adding a river theme, with fish, lotus flowers and gentle waves. The pooja plate for the river is decorated with turmeric, kumkum, flowers and a small lamp, and mango leaves are often tied at the entrance of the home.</p>
<h2 id="around-tamil-nadu-and-the-world">Around Tamil Nadu and the world</h2>
<p>The biggest celebrations take place along the Kaveri and its branches. Crowds gather at the Amma Mandapam (அம்மா மண்டபம்) ghat in Srirangam, near Tiruchirappalli, and at places such as Thiruvaiyaru, Kumbakonam, Mettur, Hogenakkal and Bhavani. Other rivers are honoured too, including the Vaigai (வைகை) and the Thamirabarani (தாமிரபரணி).</p>
<p>Tamil families abroad sometimes mark the day at a local river, lake or beach, or with a simple pooja at home. If you visit a river, please offer only flowers and natural things, and take all your plastic and leftovers home. It is a beautiful way to teach children the real message of Aadi Perukku: water is precious, and caring for our rivers is a way of thanking them.</p>9:X
